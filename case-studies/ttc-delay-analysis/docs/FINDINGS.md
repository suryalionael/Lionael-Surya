# Findings

All numbers below are generated by `R/08_findings.R` from the SQL views and
fitted model objects. None are typed by hand. The analysis covers
1,251,597 reported incidents from 2014-01-01 to 2026-09-30.

## 1. The system is mostly a bus-delay system

Bus accounts for 64.8% of incidents and 83.9% of reported delay minutes. Streetcar contributes 13.7% of incidents and 12.6% of delay minutes. Subway contributes 21.6% of incidents but only 3.5% of delay minutes. The overall share of incidents lasting 15 minutes or more is 25.7%.

## 2. Delay minutes concentrate in a few lines, especially on streetcar

The top 10 bus lines account for 17.4% of bus delay minutes, so the bus network is not line-concentrated. The top 5 streetcar lines account for 74.0% of streetcar delay minutes. YU and BD together account for 89.6% of subway delay minutes. Line 501 alone is 23.3% of streetcar delay minutes; YU is 50.3% of subway delay minutes.

## 3. The worst locations are interchange and terminal stations

Excluding the `MSTN` placeholder described below, the largest location by delay minutes is KENNEDY STATION for bus (2.5% of bus delay), DUNDAS WEST STATION for streetcar (2.9%), and EGLINTON STATION for subway (3.2%). These are places where routes meet, not isolated stops.

## 4. Major delays peak overnight, not in the PM rush

The 15-minute-plus rate is highest in the early morning: bus at 3:00 (57.3%), streetcar at 3:00 (53.4%), and subway at 5:00 (4.0%). The 15:00-19:00 window has a lower rate than midnight on bus and streetcar. In the logistic model, hour 3 has an odds ratio of 1.71 (95% CI 1.42 to 2.05) versus hour 0, while hour 15 has 0.51 (95% CI 0.44 to 0.60). The pre-specified H1 (PM peak worse than overnight) is not supported.

## 5. Operations and external causes dominate delay minutes

Bus: operations/external is 75.3% of delay minutes, equipment 21.3%, security 3.4%. Streetcar: operations/external 71.6%, equipment 17.0%, security 9.5%. Subway: operations/external 47.6%, security 26.3%, plant/infrastructure 15.1%, equipment 11.0%.

Equipment is the most common cause on bus and streetcar but not the largest source of delay minutes. Plant/infrastructure has higher odds of a major delay than equipment: 2.17 (95% CI 1.64 to 2.88). Operations/external is 1.54 (95% CI 1.27 to 1.88); security is 1.32 (95% CI 0.87 to 2.02) and its interval includes 1. H2 is partly supported, with the caveat that bus and streetcar only have coarse legacy cause labels.

## 6. Bus delays are both more frequent and more severe than subway delays

Bus has the highest major-delay rate at 34.5%, followed by streetcar at 20.5% and subway at 2.6%. In the logistic model, subway has an odds ratio of 0.05 (95% CI 0.04 to 0.05) and streetcar 0.39 (95% CI 0.39 to 0.40) relative to bus. H4 expected subway to be more severe; the data does not support that.

## 7. COVID-19 reduced incident counts, and weekends are quieter

The negative binomial model estimates a COVID-period rate ratio of 0.79 (95% CI 0.78 to 0.80), after weekday, month, mode and year. Saturday's rate ratio is 0.85 (95% CI 0.83 to 0.87) and Sunday's is 0.72 (95% CI 0.70 to 0.73). The year term is 1.01 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.01) per calendar year. H5 is supported: the COVID period is associated with lower daily counts, not higher ones.

## 8. A major delay is partly predictable, but not precisely

On the 2024 holdout the logistic model has AUC 0.756 (95% CI 0.753 to 0.759) and a Brier score of 0.1708. The mean predicted probability is 24.2% against an observed rate of 28.1%, so the model under-predicts severity in 2024. The 2024 data are harder than the 2014-2023 training window; calibration is imperfect.

## 9. Diagnostics and robustness

There is no separation: 0 fitted values are exactly 0 and 0 are exactly 1. The largest absolute coefficient is 3.07. The Poisson model has dispersion 10.09 on 13,946 residual degrees of freedom, far above 1, so the negative binomial is the appropriate count model; its theta is 9.56. Multicollinearity is high for `line_groupother` (VIF 21.5) because line groups and mode overlap; this is a limitation of the line-group specification, not a reason to drop the model.

Across the pre-registered robustness checks, the subway odds ratio ranges from 0.027 to 0.051, the plant/infrastructure odds ratio ranges from 1.53 to 3.34, and the hour-3 odds ratio ranges from 0.45 to 1.89. The 20-minute threshold attenuates the cause effects, as expected when the outcome becomes rarer, and the hour-3 association reverses below 1 at that threshold. The mode and cause directions are stable, but the hour-of-day result is sensitive to how severe a delay has to be before it counts.

## 10. Data-quality caveats that materially affect the numbers

There are 1,233 incidents with exactly 999 delay minutes (0.1% of rows), concentrated in 2018. These are almost certainly a placeholder value, not a measured delay. They inflate total delay minutes and make the `MSTN` location the largest bus location by delay; the location ranking above deliberately excludes `MSTN`. 0.08% of incidents have no usable location, and 0.08% have no mapped cause category. The cleaning reconciliation is in `outputs/tables/cleaning_reconciliation.csv`.

## What this does not show

These are associations, not causal effects. The data do not contain
ridership, weather, construction schedules, or staffing, so no result
identifies why a particular line or hour is worse after adjusting for the
variables listed in `docs/STATISTICAL_METHODS.md`. See
`docs/LIMITATIONS.md` for the full list.
